Maghrebi and Darija typing

Darija keyboard for Moroccan and Maghrebi Arabic

Type Darija with the letters standard Arabic keyboards leave out, or write Arabizi and let the converter produce Arabic script.

Darija is the Arabic dialect spoken in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, and large diaspora communities. It is written with the same Arabic script as Modern Standard Arabic, but everyday writing borrows sounds that the 28-letter core alphabet does not represent well. A phone keyboard usually forces a workaround, and a standard Arabic keyboard simply has no key for them.

This page gives those letters a home. It keeps the Arabic 101 layout you already know and adds a row for ڭ, ڨ, پ, چ, and ڥ, then pairs the keyboard with an Arabizi converter tuned for Darija vocabulary.

Why a standard Arabic keyboard is not enough

Moroccan Arabic uses ڭ for the hard g in words like ڭلس or ڭاع, and ڨ for the qaf sound in Tunisia and eastern Algeria. It also uses پ for p in loanwords such as پارك and چ for the ch sound in چاي. Most Arabic keyboard layouts omit every one of these characters. People end up writing g as غ or ق, which changes the word, or they switch to Latin letters entirely.

Having the correct letter matters when the text is read aloud, searched, or parsed. A recipe that says ڭلس is unambiguous; the same word spelled with غ may be misread as a different root.

The five extra letters

ڭg
ڨv / q
پp
چch
ڥv (Maghreb)

Use ڭ for the g sound, ڨ for the qaf used in many Tunisian and Algerian words, پ for p, چ for ch, and ڥ for the alternative Maghrebi v. All five are clickable below the editor and appear as normal Unicode characters once copied.

Arabizi to Arabic in one space bar

Many people write Darija in Latin script: mrhba, labas, bzaf, wach, kifach, 3afak, 9alb. Switching to the keyboard's Arabizi mode makes that habit productive. Type the word, press Space, and it becomes Arabic script.

The converter treats the digits as letters: 2 is ء, 3 is ع, 5 is خ, 6 is ط, 7 is ح, and 9 is ق. It knows that Darija g is usually ڭ rather than غ, that v is usually ڨ, and that ch is ش. Frequent words such as mzyan, safi, daba, and inshallah are converted as whole words instead of letter by letter.

If a word comes out wrong, switch to Latin mode, correct it with normal letters, then switch back. The converter does not learn from corrections, but the mode switch is instant.

Typing with a physical keyboard

In layout mode, your keyboard behaves like a standard Arabic keyboard: Q is ض, W is ص, A is ش, and so on. The extra Maghrebi letters are available on the on-screen row, which is often faster than remembering a custom shortcut. On a phone or tablet, tap the virtual keys directly.

The harakat row works for Darija too. Most everyday Darija is written without vocalization, but religious phrases and teaching material often need a fatha, damma, or shadda, and the editor keeps those marks in the correct order.

Copying text into other apps

Press Copy and paste into WhatsApp, Instagram, a Word document, or a subtitle editor. The characters are standard Unicode, so they do not depend on this site or on a special font. If an app displays disconnected letters, run the line through the Arabic text fixer for Photoshop before pasting.

Frequently asked questions

Which letters does the Darija keyboard add?

It adds ڭ for g, ڨ for the Tunisian and Algerian qaf, پ for p, چ for ch, and ڥ as an alternative v. They appear in an extra row under the main Arabic layout.

Is Darija written differently from Arabic?

It uses the same script and most of the same letters, but adds a few characters for sounds that do not exist in Modern Standard Arabic. Word order and vocabulary also differ, which is why the Arabizi dictionary includes Darija words.

Can I type Arabizi with numbers?

Yes. In Arabizi mode, 2 becomes ء, 3 becomes ع, 5 becomes خ, 6 becomes ط, 7 becomes ح, and 9 becomes ق. Type the word, then press Space to convert it.

Does this keyboard work on a phone?

Yes. Tap the virtual keys, including the extra Maghrebi letters and harakat. The layout is responsive and the editor keeps the text right-to-left.

Can I use the result in Instagram or WhatsApp?

Yes. Copy produces ordinary Unicode text, so it pastes correctly into social apps, messaging apps, email, and word processors.
