An Arabic keyboard you can use right now solves a very specific problem: the computer in front of you has no Arabic layout, no Arabic language pack, and no administrator rights to install one. This page puts a full Arabic keyboard in the browser. You click the letters, or you map your existing physical keyboard to Arabic, and the text appears in the editor above.

Everything runs locally as JavaScript. The text is not sent to a server, not saved to an account, and not uploaded anywhere. When you close the tab, it is gone. That makes the page safe for private messages, client documents, subtitles, and homework alike.

How to type Arabic online

  1. Click inside the editor so the cursor is active.
  2. Pick a mode: Arabic layout for a normal Arabic keyboard, Arabizi to type Latin letters and convert them, or Latin to type English and numbers normally.
  3. Type with the on-screen keys, or use your own keyboard. In layout mode, the letters on your physical keys produce Arabic characters.
  4. Add harakat from the gold row when you need vocalization, then press Copy and paste the result into any app.

The character counter under the editor updates as you type, so you always know how much text you have before pasting it into a field with a limit.

Three typing modes for three habits

People arrive at an Arabic keyboard from very different starting points. The mode selector covers all three.

  • Arabic layout is the familiar 101 layout. If you have ever used an Arabic keyboard, the keys will feel correct: ض on Q, ش on A, ي on D, and so on. Use it when you can touch-type Arabic or when you want the on-screen keys to match a physical Arabic keyboard.
  • Arabizi lets you write the way many people text: marhaba, kifak, 3afak, 9alb. Press Space and the word is converted to Arabic script. Numbers stand in for letters that have no Latin equivalent, and common words are recognized as whole units.
  • Latin turns the conversion off. Use it for an English sentence, a phone number, or a URL inside otherwise Arabic text.

Smart vowel handling is on by default. It avoids the classic transliteration mess where every Latin a becomes an alef and every e becomes a ya. Instead, it uses the position of the vowel in the word, common consonant clusters, and the end of the word to choose a sensible Arabic spelling.

Adding harakat without breaking the word

Arabic diacritics are marks, not letters. They attach to the letter before them, and only one short vowel can sit on a letter at a time. The editor enforces those rules as you type:

  • A second vowel on the same letter replaces the first one instead of stacking.
  • Shadda is placed before the vowel it doubles, so مُحَمَّد comes out in the right order.
  • Shadda plus sukun collapses to the more useful combination rather than producing an unreadable pair.

If you paste vocalized text from elsewhere, the same cleanup runs on the pasted content. That is useful when a document mixes several sources and the mark order has drifted.

Common Arabic typing problems

Most complaints about typing Arabic come from four issues, and none of them require reinstalling Windows or macOS.

No Arabic language pack

Use this page and copy the result. A browser keyboard does not change your system settings, so it works on locked-down work computers and school laptops.

Letters look disconnected after pasting

Some design programs, especially older Photoshop versions, do not shape Arabic text correctly. They show isolated letter forms instead of joined words. The Photoshop Arabic text fixer converts a line into presentation forms in visual order, which those programs can display correctly.

Numbers and English words jump around

Arabic is right-to-left, but numbers and Latin words inside it are left-to-right. Browsers usually handle this with the Unicode bidirectional algorithm. When a field or app does not, keep Latin fragments on their own line or add a neutral character such as a space around them.

Commas and question marks look wrong

Arabic uses ، and ؟ rather than , and ?. The punctuation converter swaps them without touching decimal separators, and the text cleaner fixes spacing around them.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Is this Arabic keyboard free to use?

Yes. Every keyboard and text tool on the site is free, with no account, no trial period, and no download. The page is a static site, so there is nothing to install.

Do I need to install an Arabic language pack?

No. The keyboard runs in the browser and produces normal Unicode Arabic text. You can paste that text into Word, Google Docs, WhatsApp, email, or any other app without changing your system language settings.

Does the site store or upload my text?

No. Conversion happens in JavaScript on your device. The editor is cleared when you reload or close the page, and there is no account or database behind the tools.

Can I use my physical keyboard to type Arabic?

Yes. Choose Arabic layout and type on your normal keyboard; the letters are mapped to the standard Arabic 101 positions. You can also click the on-screen keys, which is easier on a phone or tablet.

Why does pasted Arabic look broken in Photoshop?

Older design software does not apply Arabic shaping. Use the Photoshop text fixer on this site to convert the line into connected presentation forms before pasting it into a text layer.

Can I type Moroccan Darija or Persian here?

Yes. The Darija keyboard adds ڭ, ڨ, پ, چ, and ڥ, while the Persian keyboard adds پ, چ, ژ, and گ. Each has its own transliteration rules and on-screen layout.
