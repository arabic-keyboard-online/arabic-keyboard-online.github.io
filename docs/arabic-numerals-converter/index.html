Eastern and Western digits

Arabic numerals converter

Switch a whole document between 0–9 and ٠–٩ without retyping a single number.

Converted text

Only digits change. Letters, punctuation, spaces, and the plus sign in phone numbers stay as they are.

Everything runs locally in your browser. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

Arabic text can use two sets of digits. Western digits (0–9) are common in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, and much of the web. Arabic-Indic digits (٠–٩) are standard in Egypt, the Gulf, and many print publications. Persian adds a third set (۰–۹). None of them is more correct; the right choice depends on the audience and the publication style.

This converter changes digits only. It leaves letters, punctuation, spaces, currency symbols, and phone-number formatting where they are, so you can switch a paragraph or an invoice without rebuilding it.

The tool converts Western digits to Arabic-Indic and back. Persian digits are accepted as input on the way to Western, because they share the same positions but use different code points.

How to convert

  1. Paste the text into the left editor.
  2. Choose the direction: Western to Arabic-Indic, or Arabic-Indic to Western.
  3. Read the result on the right and press Copy.

The result updates as you type. The status line reports how many digits were converted, so you can tell at a glance whether the text contained numbers at all.

Mixed digits in one document

If a document mixes both systems, run it in one direction first, then fix exceptions manually. A single direction pass turns every digit, including years, prices, and phone numbers. If a phone number must keep Western digits for dialing, copy it out before converting and paste it back afterward, or keep the number on a separate line and convert only the body text.

Digits and search

Search engines usually normalize digit variants, but not always for Arabic queries. A product code written with Arabic-Indic digits may not match a user who types the same code with Western digits. If the number is an identifier, keep the form that matches the original. If it is prose, choose the form your audience expects and stay consistent within the page.

What does not change

Only the ten digit characters are replaced. Decimal separators, thousands separators, the Arabic decimal separator ٫, time colons, slashes in dates, and the plus sign in international phone numbers are untouched. That keeps the structure of the number intact while changing its script.

Currency: if the amount also needs words, use the tafqit converter after converting the digits, or write the amount in words directly.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

What are Arabic-Indic digits?

They are the digits ٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩ used in Arabic-speaking countries. They represent the same values as 0–9 but are different Unicode characters.

Can I convert in both directions?

Yes. Choose Western to Arabic-Indic or Arabic-Indic to Western. Persian digits are also accepted when converting to Western.

Does it change punctuation or phone numbers?

It changes every digit it finds, but it leaves punctuation, spaces, and symbols alone. A phone number with a plus sign keeps the plus; only its digits change.

Which digit style should I use?

Match your audience. Western digits are common in the Maghreb and online; Arabic-Indic digits are standard in Egypt, the Gulf, and much print media. Consistency matters more than the choice.

Will converting break dates or times?

The separators stay the same, so a date such as 21/11/2026 becomes ٢١/١١/٢٠٢٦ with the same structure. Check the result if the destination app expects a specific digit set.
