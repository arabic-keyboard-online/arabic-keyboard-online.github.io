Bulk Arabic editing

Arabic find and replace

Change a name, fix a repeated typo, or rename a term everywhere in the text with one controlled replacement.

Matching
Result

Everything runs locally in your browser. Nothing is uploaded to a server.

Editing Arabic text often means changing the same term in a dozen places: a company name, a date, a spelling that the client prefers, or a word that was translated two different ways. Doing it by eye invites missed instances and accidental edits.

This tool replaces every occurrence in one pass and shows you the result before you copy it. It supports case-sensitive matching, whole-word matching, and plain text replacement. Special characters in the search term are escaped, so a dot or a bracket is treated as text rather than as a regular expression.

How to replace text

  1. Paste the source text into the big editor.
  2. Type the word or phrase to find. Arabic, English, and mixed terms are all accepted.
  3. Type the replacement, or leave it empty to delete every match.
  4. Choose whether to match case and whether to match whole words.
  5. Press Replace all, check the result, then copy it.

The result stays in a separate read-only box, so the original is preserved until you copy the new version.

Whole words and fragments

Arabic attaches prefixes and suffixes to words: و, ال, ب, ل, ك, and possessive endings. If you search for كتاب with whole-word matching on, a word such as الكتاب may not match, depending on the word boundary. Turn whole-word matching off when you want to catch those attached forms.

With whole-word matching off, a search for a short term can also match inside a longer word. Check the count and skim the result before copying. The tool reports how many replacements it made, which helps you decide whether the scope is right.

Case and mixed scripts

Arabic has no letter case, but a mixed document may contain English names, product codes, or URLs. Case-sensitive matching applies to those Latin characters. If the source mixes Photoshop and photoshop, leave case matching off to replace both spellings with one chosen form.

Literal text, not regex

The search term is escaped before it becomes a pattern. That means 3.14 matches the literal characters, not “3, any character, 14”. It also means a search for a bracket or a question mark works without special syntax. This is deliberate: most Arabic editing tasks are literal, and a surprise regex is a good way to damage a document.

Common uses

  • Updating a brand name after a merger.
  • Standardizing one spelling of a place name across a report.
  • Removing a repeated phrase that a translation tool inserted.
  • Changing Western digits to Arabic-Indic digits in a specific context.
  • Replacing a placeholder such as [الاسم] with a real name.

For punctuation-only changes, the punctuation converter is safer because it understands decimals. For whitespace, use the text cleaner.

Local editing

Replacement happens in your browser with JavaScript. No text is uploaded, and no history is kept. Copy the result before reloading the page.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

Does replace all work with Arabic text?

Yes. Arabic letters, harakat, punctuation, and mixed English terms are all supported. The search is plain text, so special characters are matched literally.

What does whole words only do?

It adds Unicode word boundaries around the search term, so a match must be a complete word. Turn it off to catch prefixes, suffixes, and fragments inside longer words.

Can I delete a word instead of replacing it?

Yes. Leave the replacement field empty and press Replace all. Every match is removed, including the surrounding spaces only where they are part of the match.

Does it support regular expressions?

No. The search term is treated as literal text and escaped automatically. That prevents accidental pattern behavior with dots, brackets, and question marks.

Is the original text preserved?

Yes. The source stays in the input box and the result appears in a separate read-only box until you copy it or clear the editor.
